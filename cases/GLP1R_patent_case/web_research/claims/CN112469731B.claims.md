GIP/GLP1 co-agonist compounds
The present invention relates to compounds active on both human glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptors. The invention also relates to compounds having an extended duration of action at each of these receptors. In addition, the present invention relates to compounds that can be administered orally. The compounds are useful for the treatment of type 2 diabetes ("T2 DM"). Furthermore, the compounds are useful for the treatment of obesity.

The prevalence of diabetes continues to rise over the past few decades. T2DM is the most common form of diabetes, accounting for about 90% of all diabetes. T2DM is characterized by high blood glucose levels, which are mainly associated with insulin resistance. Current standards of care for T2DM include diet and exercise, treatment with oral medications and injectable glucose-lowering medications (including incretin-based therapies such as GLP-1 receptor agonists). There are a number of GLP-1 receptor agonists currently available for the treatment of T2DM, although currently commercially available GLP-1 receptor agonists are generally limited in dosage due to gastrointestinal side effects such as nausea and vomiting. Subcutaneous injections are typical routes of administration for available GLP-1 receptor agonists. Insulin therapy is considered when insufficient treatment with oral medications and incretins-based therapies. Despite the advances in the treatments available today, many patients with T2DM fail to achieve their glycemic control goals. Uncontrolled diabetes results in several conditions associated with increased morbidity and mortality in patients. There is a need for therapies that enable more T2DM patients to reach their glycemic therapeutic targets.

Obesity is a complex medical condition that results in excessive aggregation of adipose tissue mass. Obesity is today a global public health problem that is associated with undesirable health consequences and morbidity. The desired treatment for obese patients strives to reduce overweight, ameliorate obesity-related complications, and maintain long-term weight loss. The available obesity treatments are especially unsatisfactory for severely obese patients. Alternative treatment options are needed to cause therapeutic weight loss in patients in need of such treatment.

WO2016/111971 describes peptides which are said to have GLP-1 and GIP activity. WO2013/164483 also discloses compounds which are said to have GLP-1 and GIP activity.

There is a need for T2DM therapies that can provide effective glucose control to a large proportion of patients in need of such treatment. There is also a need for T2D therapies that provide effective glucose control and have a favorable side effect profile. Alternative treatment options are needed to provide therapeutic weight loss to patients in need of such treatment. For patients in need of treatment for severe obesity, alternative treatment options are needed.

Compounds having agonist activity at GIP and GLP-1 receptors and suitable for oral administration are desired. Compounds with prolonged duration of action on each of the GIP and GLP-1 receptors are needed to allow for less frequency of administration.
